# Brand Direction — Adv3D B2B Contract Manufacturing

**Audience:** procurement leads, manufacturing engineers, ops managers, design studios, engineering firms, product-dev agencies (NOT hobbyists, NOT creators, NOT startups).
**Tone:** confident, sober, technical, credentialing. Procurement-grade skepticism. No playfulness.
**Family affinity:** this page must read as the same family as `opservices.advanc3dinc.com/` — same orange, same editorial restraint, same credible-industrial feel — but B2B-stiffer (no O&P warmth, no patient language, more production-floor voice).

---

## 1. Tone and voice

**Voice:** senior procurement engineer talking to another senior procurement engineer. No marketing varnish. No "we're passionate about." No exclamation marks. Declarative sentences. Second person ("your production line", "your margin layer"). Specific nouns over abstract virtues — name the material, name the process, name the tolerance.

**Tone spectrum:**
- Far from: playful, lifestyle, aspirational, emotional, founder-story-driven
- Close to: technical manual, RFQ reply, capability statement, NDA preamble
- Reference point: a Tier 1 auto supplier's "Manufacturing Capabilities" PDF — sober, dense, credentialing — translated into web language without losing the gravitas

**Sentence shape:**
- Short declaratives in body copy. Long sentences only in the hero subhead and the PROBLEM section where the agitation needs room.
- No rhetorical questions outside the FAQ.
- No "we" without a specific capability attached. ("We run FDM, SLA, SLS, and MJF on a coordinated shop floor" is fine; "We are passionate about innovation" is forbidden.)

**Headline voice (hero):**
Outcome-first, three-clause format: `[Result]. [Result]. [Result].`
Example shape (to be finalized by copywriter, per COPY-01): "Production-ready parts on your dock. Spec-compliant repeatability. No engineering babysitting."
- Each clause is a specific, falsifiable claim — not a vibe.
- No "Welcome to." No "Unlock." No "Discover."

## 3. Use-case examples (two customer tracks)

The brief calls for two distinct B2B lead types served from one page. Design-wise, both tracks share a single visual system but split cleanly at the WHAT YOU GET section and the FINAL CTA.

### Track A — OEM / production manufacturing
- **Buyer:** procurement lead at a Tier 1 auto supplier, appliance OEM, industrial equipment brand, or aerospace component shop.
- **Trigger:** needs a vendor who can take a print-ready CAD file (or even a STEP / IGES) and ship spec-compliant parts at volume — not a hobbyist printer, not a "we'll figure it out" shop.
- **Hero relevance:** the production-grade / tolerances / repeatable output cluster.
- **CTA destination:** "Request a Contract Manufacturing Quote" (primary).
- **Reading path:** hero → problem (sourcing pain) → why us (production discipline) → what you get (OEM deliverables) → how it works → proof (scaffold: capability examples available on request) → FAQ (lead time / QA / materials) → final CTA.

### Track B — White-label / digital foundry partner
- **Buyer:** design studio principal, engineering firm partner, product-dev agency owner, reseller.
- **Trigger:** needs a silent manufacturing partner behind their client work — no co-branding on packaging, no client poaching, no margin leakage.
- **Hero relevance:** the white-label / NDA / margin-layer partnership cluster. The same hero speaks to both tracks; the WHAT YOU GET section splits cleanly into "for OEMs" and "for white-label partners."
- **CTA destination:** "Explore White-Label Partnership" (secondary).
- **Reading path:** hero → problem → why us → what you get (white-label column) → how it works (same path; NDA flagged in step 1) → proof (scaffold) → FAQ (NDA / IP / client relationships — these are the white-label track's emotional core) → final CTA.

**Design implication:** WHAT YOU GET is the only section that materially diverges by track. It must be a true 2-column split (NOT a 3-column symmetric grid). One column titled for OEMs, one for white-label partners, each with a tight list of deliverables in that column's vocabulary.

## 4. Brand differentiators

What makes Adv3D distinct from the "another 3D printing shop" category. These are the claims WHY US should land on.

1. **Production discipline over prototype-grade variance.** Run-to-run QA documented. Tolerances confirmed per batch. Procurement-grade buyers have been burned by prototype vendors who can't hold ±0.2mm at volume — this is the credibility cliff and Adv3D owns it.
2. **Engineering-first intake.** Files are reviewed and DFM-flagged before the print job starts. The shop tells you the design won't hold, not after you've paid for 200 parts.
3. **Multi-process / multi-material floor.** FDM, SLA, SLS, powder-bed, multi-durometer, flexible material, large-format. The shop picks the right process per part instead of "we run FDM, that's what we have."
4. **White-label discretion as a first-class track, not an afterthought.** NDA at intake, no co-marketing, no client poaching, no logo on packaging unless the partner requests it. The shop is invisible to the partner's clients. This is the margin-layer partnership.
5. **US-based production.** Important for buyers with supply-chain concerns (ITAR-adjacent industries, defense, medical-adjacent). Not a "made in China with US shipping" operation.

**What this brand is NOT:**
- NOT a hobbyist / creator / maker-shop funnel (different site, different audience, different page)
- NOT a "we'll print anything you send us" commodity shop
- NOT a consulting-first firm that outsources the actual production

## 5. What makes this brand distinct from competitors

The direct competitor set for B2B contract manufacturing in additive is: Xometry, Protolabs, Fictiv, Hubs, Shapeways (enterprise tier), smaller regional shops.

**Where Adv3D wins the design frame:**
- **vs Xometry / Protolabs (the aggregators):** Advanc3D is a real shop floor with a real engineering team, not an instant-quoting marketplace. The page should feel like a manufacturer, not a quoting widget. No "upload your STL and get a price in 30 seconds" hero. No giant file-upload UI.
- **vs Fictiv (the curated network):** Advanc3D is the actual production floor, not a curated broker. The white-label track is something aggregators can't credibly offer because they aggregate.
- **vs small regional shops:** Advanc3D is multi-process and at volume. The page should not look like a 2-person machine shop's homepage — it should look like a Tier 1 supplier's capability statement.
- **vs the parent site (`opservices.advanc3dinc.com`):** The parent site sells O&P fabrication to clinicians — warm, patient-aware, premium healthcare. The B2B page sells production capacity to procurement leads — sober, technical, spec-sheet-credible. Same orange, same family, different gravitational pull.

**Visual signal that distinguishes:** editorial serif display (the parent site uses serif/sans pairing; the B2B page keeps the pairing but pushes the serif toward *industrial editorial* rather than *medical editorial*). No patient language, no clinical language. Production-floor vocabulary throughout.

## 6. Words and phrases to avoid (additive to canonical list)

**Canonical 24 forbidden phrases** (per `codex-copywriter/SOUL.md` and `codex-qa/SOUL.md`) — already enforced. Common offenders that will trip the grep: "all-in-one", "seamless", "empower", "unlock", "journey", "solution", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "revolutionize", "transform".

**Q10 inversions (REQUIRED for this build, EXEMPT from grep):** `aerospace`, `automotive`, `industrial`, `production-grade`, `engineering-grade`, `end-use`. These must appear at least once each in rendered copy.

**B2B-specific avoid list (this brief, additive):**
- "partner with us on your journey" — too lifestyle, too creator-funnel
- "we'd love to chat" / "let's connect" — too startup
- "world-class" / "industry-leading" / "best-in-class" — procurement-grade buyers ignore these as decoration
- "innovative solutions" / "next-generation" — empty
- "passionate about" / "love what we do" — irrelevant for procurement
- "from concept to production" — overused on every 3D-printing site; the HOW IT WORKS section must be more specific than this
- "one-stop shop" / "full-service" — implies aggregation, not direct manufacture
- "your trusted partner" — empty
- "let's build something amazing together" — creator-funnel language, forbidden
- "rapid prototyping" alone (without qualification) — implies prototype-grade output, which contradicts the production-grade positioning
- "competitive pricing" — commodity framing; this page is a capability statement, not a price-comparison tool

**B2B-specific USE list (this brief, additive):**
- "spec-compliant" / "tolerance-confirmed"
- "production volume" / "production-grade"
- "repeatable output" / "run-to-run QA"
- "engineering review" / "DFM-flagged"
- "white-label" / "margin-layer partnership"
- "NDA at intake" / "partner confidentiality"
- "named processes" — FDM, SLA, SLS, MJF, powder-bed, multi-durometer — not "additive manufacturing" alone
- "delivery windows" / "lead times" with specific framings (not just "fast turnaround")

## 7. Design anti-patterns (what the visual gate will fail)

Visual decisions that must not appear on the rendered page:

- No gradient buttons (solid `brand.primary` only — checked by `grep -r bg-gradient`)
- No 3-column symmetric feature grid (no `grid-cols-3` with three equal items)
- No icons inside colored circles (no `bg-orange-500 rounded-full` decorative circles around icons)
- No colored left-border accent on cards (no `border-l-4 border-orange-500`)
- No centered-everything hero (hero must be 2-column asymmetric, left text + right image)
- No placeholder text visible anywhere (no "Lorem ipsum", no "TODO", no "your logo here")
- No fake testimonials, no lorem-ipsum case studies, no invented client logos (PROOF section is honest scaffold)
- No hotlinked images — every `<Image>` src resolves to a local file in `public/assets/` or `research/images/`
- No "Learn More" / "Get Started" / "Click Here" CTA text (CTA must be action + outcome)
- No identical `py-*` across all sections (hero + proof get the most breathing room; process + FAQ run tighter)

## 9. North-star check (the visual gate's final question)

Before approving the build, ask: **"Does this page look like it would be sent in an email by a Tier 1 auto supplier's procurement lead to a colleague, with the subject line 'capability statement for our additive manufacturing partner'?"**

If yes → pass.
If it looks like it would be sent by a hobbyist creator with "check out my new printer!" → fail.
